# Style: Ballad

## Hard rules
- Tells a story: a ballad is narrative first, with a plot, characters, and usually a dramatic or tragic event
- Written in quatrains (four-line stanzas)
- **Ballad meter:** alternating lines of four stresses and three stresses (iambic tetrameter / trimeter, "common meter" is 8.6.8.6 syllables)
- Rhyme scheme ABCB (lines 2 and 4 rhyme). ABAB is also acceptable

## Conventions
- **Starts in the middle of things**, with little backstory. Jump between scenes without transitions
- **Dialogue** carries much of the action, often without "he said" tags
- **Refrains** and **incremental repetition**: a line or stanza repeats with small changes that advance the story (e.g. question-and-answer stanzas that reveal a poisoning or murder one step at a time)
- **Impersonal narrator:** the teller rarely comments on or explains the emotion, and lets events speak
- Stock phrases and formulas ("milk-white steed," "blood-red wine") and symbolic numbers (three, seven)
- Ends in a striking image or the plain statement of a fate
- The literary ballad (Coleridge, Keats, Wilde) borrows these features with more polish and interiority

## Themes & imagery
Tragic love, betrayal, murder and revenge, shipwreck and disaster, the supernatural (ghosts, revenants, fairy abductions), outlaws and heroes, war, and working lives (miners, sailors, soldiers).

## Tone & vocabulary
Plain, direct, and oral: built to be sung or recited and remembered. Concrete nouns and strong verbs. Keep the rhythm regular enough to sing. Suspense comes from what's left unsaid.
