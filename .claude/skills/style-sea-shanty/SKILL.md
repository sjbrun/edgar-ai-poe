# Style: Sea Shanty

## Hard rules
- **Built for group singing.** A shantyman sings the solo lines and the crew answers with a refrain. Every song needs a refrain or chorus a crowd can pick up after hearing it once
- **The refrain repeats exactly**, word for word, every time. The story lives in the solo lines, and the refrain doesn't change
- **A strong, steady beat** that matches a physical action (a pull, a push, a stamp). Read it aloud and stress the beats. The rhythm has to work for hauling or marching
- **Short, singable lines** with simple end-rhymes (usually couplets, or ABCB in longer verses). Near-rhymes are fine ("Liverpool / school," "brine / Line")

## Conventions

### Types (choose one, and say which in the notes)
| Type | Job | Structure | Example in examples.md |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Short-drag (short-haul)** | A few sharp pulls on a line | One solo line, then one fixed refrain with a single shouted pull-word ("…the bowline **HAUL**!") | Haul on the Bowline |
| **Halyard** | Long, repeated hauls to raise a sail | Alternating: solo line, crew refrain, solo line, crew refrain. Two pulls per verse, landing on the refrain's stressed words | The Black Ball Line |
| **Capstan / windlass** | Steady walking round the capstan | Longer narrative verses and a full chorus. Can include **echo answers** where the crew repeats the end of each solo line in parentheses | Paddy Lay Back |
| **Forebitter** | Off-duty songs sung for entertainment, not work | Ballad-like, with long storytelling lines and a chorus. Freer rhythm | Off to Sea Once More, All for me Grog |

If the user just says "sea shanty," default to a **halyard** or **capstan** shanty with a clear call-and-response refrain.

### Devices
- **Vocables and calls in the refrain:** "way, hey," "to me way-hey-hey," "oh rah oh," "heave away," "haul away," "hurrah"
- **Echo answers** in parentheses: "And all of me money it was spent (it was spent)." Echoes can break up words for comic effect ("(vance vance)")
- **List structure:** each verse fills one slot in a repeating frame ("Where is me boots… / Where is me shirt… / Where is me bed…", each "All gone for beer and tobacco")
- **Incremental verses:** one fixed opening phrase with a new line each time ("Haul on the bowline, …"), so a song can be extended on the spot
- **Circular structure:** the last verse repeats or echoes the first ("I'll sing you a song of…" → "I've sung my song of…")
- **Direct address:** "me lads," "me boys," "me bully boys," "come all you bold seafarin' men…"
- **Shouted or drawn-out moments** for the crew: "heave a pawwwwwl!", "the bowline HAUL!"
- **A "moral" or warning** at the end of a forebitter, often ironic ("Take my advice…")

## Themes & imagery
- **The sailor's cycle:** ashore with pay, money gone, cheated or broke, forced back to sea ("outward bound again")
- **Shipping-life machinery:** the shipping office, the month's advance, crimps and boarding-house masters, being shanghaied, paying off
- **Hardship at sea:** cold, storms, rounding the Horn, whaling, bad food, harsh officers ("the old man" = captain, "the mate")
- **Ports and routes:** Liverpool, London, 'Frisco, Valparaiso, Greenland, the "western ocean," the Horn
- **Ships and lines:** naming and bragging about a ship or shipping line ("the finest ships that sailed the brine")
- **Drink, women ashore, and sweethearts:** grog, rum, beer and tobacco, a girl in port ("Kitty lives in Liverpool")
- **Sea vocabulary:** haul, heave, bowline, capstan, pawl, halyard, slack, barque, quarterdeck, watch, stern, brine, knots

## Tone & vocabulary
- **Rough, cheerful, and self-mocking.** Even misery gets a wry grin. The sailor is usually the butt of the joke (drunk, robbed, broke, back at sea)
- **Working-class sailor dialect:** "me" for "my," dropped g's ("mornin'," "a-rollin'"), "'twas," "a-" prefixes ("a-growlin'," "a-walking"), "afore"
- **Plain, concrete, physical language.** No abstract or literary vocabulary
- **Bawdiness:** traditional shanties are often earthy and crude. Match the user's request. Default to mild, family-friendly innuendo unless they ask for the raw version
- When writing new shanties, use the traditional flavor without copying existing traditional verses wholesale. Fresh verses on familiar frames are true to the tradition
