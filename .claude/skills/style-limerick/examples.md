# Limerick Examples

## Example 1 (Edward Lear, *A Book of Nonsense*, 1846, public domain)
```
There was an Old Man with a beard,
Who said, "It is just as I feared!—
Two Owls and a Hen,
Four Larks and a Wren,
Have all built their nests in my beard!"
```
**Why it works:** It follows a clean AABBA scheme. The short lines 3 and 4 pile up a list of birds, and the absurd image lands in line 5. It also shows Lear's habit of reusing line 1's end word ("beard") in the last line.

## Example 2 (original)
```
A cat who was fond of the sea
Set sail in a boat built for three;
But the crew (two old mice)
Found the voyage less nice
When they learned they were part of the tea.
```
**Why it works:** The anapestic bounce holds throughout ("a CAT who was FOND of the SEA"). Lines 3 and 4 set up the mice, and line 5 uses a fresh rhyme to reveal the twist: they're the meal.

## Example 3 (Edward Lear, *A Book of Nonsense*, 1846, public domain)
```
There was an Old Man who said, "Hush!
I perceive a young bird in this bush!"
When they said, "Is it small?"
He replied, "Not at all!
It is four times as big as the bush!"
```
**Why it works:** Dialogue drives the joke. Lines 3 and 4 are a quick question and answer, and line 5 delivers a size that makes no sense (a bird bigger than the bush it hides in). Lear again repeats an end word ("bush"), and the nonsense comes from logic, not wordplay.

## Example 4 (attributed to Cosmo Monkhouse, late 19th century, public domain)
```
There was a young lady of Niger
Who smiled as she rode on a tiger;
They returned from the ride
With the lady inside,
And the smile on the face of the tiger.
```
**Why it works:** The final line is a twist told by implication: it never says the tiger ate her, only that the smile has moved. The feminine rhymes (Niger / tiger) add bounce, and the dark joke is told calmly. It's the post-Lear model, where the last line brings a new turn instead of repeating line 1.

## Example 5 (A. H. Reginald Buller, *Punch*, 1923, public domain)
```
There was a young lady named Bright
Whose speed was far faster than light;
She set out one day
In a relative way
And returned on the previous night.
```
**Why it works:** A pun ("relative way," after Einstein's relativity) in line 4 leads to an impossible payoff in line 5. It shows the limerick used for clever, topical humor. The anapestic rhythm is perfectly regular, which keeps the science joke light.
