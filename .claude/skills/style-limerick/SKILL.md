# Style: Limerick

## Hard rules
- Five lines with an AABBA rhyme scheme
- Lines 1, 2, and 5 are long, with three stressed beats each (usually 8–9 syllables)
- Lines 3 and 4 are short, with two stressed beats each (usually 5–6 syllables)
- Anapestic meter (da-da-DUM), often starting with a dropped first syllable: "a CAT who was FOND of the SEA"

## Conventions
- Line 1 introduces a character, often with a place: "There once was a…" or "There was an Old Man of…"
- Lines 3 and 4 build the complication quickly
- Line 5 delivers the punchline, with a twist, pun, or surprise rhyme
- Edward Lear's older style repeats line 1's end word in line 5. Modern limericks use a fresh rhyme word for a sharper punch
- Strained or invented rhymes are part of the fun when they're obviously deliberate

## Themes & imagery
Absurd situations, eccentric people, mishaps, animals, food, and mild bodily comedy. Place names supply rhymes.

## Tone & vocabulary
Playful, quick, and light. Plain words with a bouncy rhythm. Avoid serious subjects unless the humor comes from the contrast. The meter must bounce, so read every line aloud and fix any stumbles.
