# Style: Acrostic

## Hard rules
- The first letters of the lines, read top to bottom, spell a word, name, or phrase (the *key word*)
- One line per letter, in order, with no skipped or extra lines
- The spelled word must relate to the poem's subject, usually the subject itself or the person it's addressed to

### Variants
- **Mesostic:** the key word runs down the middle of the lines
- **Telestich:** the key word is spelled by the last letters
- **Double acrostic:** both the first and last letters spell words
- **Abecedarian:** the lines start with each letter of the alphabet in order

Default to a standard first-letter acrostic unless the user asks for a variant.

## Conventions
- Meter and rhyme are optional. Acrostics can be free verse or rhymed (couplets work well)
- The best acrostics read naturally as poems, so the reader shouldn't feel the letter constraint. Don't let every line become a single adjective ("Happy / Amazing / Radiant…") unless the request is for a children's or simple acrostic
- Run sentences across lines so the poem has a flow and an arc, not just a list
- Plan the hard letters first (Q, X, Z, Y, K): "Xylophone," "eXtra," "Zero," "Yet," "Quietly," "Kindly." Words like "Zealous," "Yearning," "eXactly" can help, or reword the line so it starts with a short connecting word
- Show the acrostic letters with bold or capitals when presenting the poem, unless the user wants the hidden word kept hidden

## Themes & imagery
Often personal or occasional: names (gifts, birthdays, love poems), places, seasons, holidays, or concepts. Historically used for hidden dedications, puzzles, and devotional verse.

## Tone & vocabulary
Anything from playful to tender to solemn, matched to the occasion. For names of real people, keep the tone warm and use details the user provides.
