# Style: Villanelle

## Hard rules
- **19 lines:** five tercets (three-line stanzas) followed by a quatrain
- **Only two rhyme sounds** in the whole poem: rhyme scheme ABA ABA ABA ABA ABA ABAA
- **Two refrains:** the first and third lines of the poem (A1 and A2) rhyme with each other and repeat on a fixed schedule:

```
Tercet 1:  A1  b  A2
Tercet 2:  a   b  A1
Tercet 3:  a   b  A2
Tercet 4:  a   b  A1
Tercet 5:  a   b  A2
Quatrain:  a   b  A1  A2
```

- Meter isn't fixed, but iambic pentameter is the English norm. Keep all lines about the same length

## Conventions
- **Choose the refrains first.** They appear 4 times each and must work as a closing pair, so write them before anything else
- Each time a refrain returns, the context should change its meaning: it should feel inevitable but also mean something new
- Small variations in the refrains (punctuation, one changed word) are allowed and often powerful, especially in the final quatrain
- The form's circling repetition suits obsession, grief, insistence, and things that can't be escaped or let go
- The b-rhyme needs 6 words, and the a-rhyme needs 13 (counting the refrains). Pick rhyme sounds with plenty of options (-ight, -ay, -ain, -ow)

## Themes & imagery
Loss, death, obsession, memory, longing, and defiance. Well-known modern examples (Dylan Thomas's "Do not go gentle into that good night," Elizabeth Bishop's "One Art") are still under copyright: describe their techniques, but don't quote them.

## Tone & vocabulary
Intense and controlled at once. The strict form holds back strong emotion. Keep the syntax natural so the refrains don't feel bolted on.
