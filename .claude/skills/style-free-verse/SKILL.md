# Style: Free Verse

## Hard rules
- No fixed meter or regular rhyme scheme. If it scans and rhymes regularly, it isn't free verse
- Every line break must be a deliberate choice. Free verse still has form, but the poem creates it instead of inheriting it

## Conventions
- **Line breaks** create emphasis, pause, and double meaning. Ending a line on a strong word gives it weight. Enjambment can briefly suggest one meaning before the next line completes another
- **Cadence** replaces meter: the rhythm of natural speech, breath units, or phrases
- **Repetition and parallelism** (anaphora, lists, catalogues) give structure in place of rhyme, as in Whitman
- **Sound** still matters: assonance, consonance, internal and occasional rhyme
- **White space**, stanza breaks, indentation, and line length all carry meaning
- Lines can range from one word to long prose-like sweeps. Vary them on purpose

## Themes & imagery
Anything. Free verse suits close observation, the inner life, everyday moments, and political or confessional material. Concrete, specific images are stronger than abstract statements, so show more than you tell.

## Tone & vocabulary
Usually conversational and contemporary, close to natural speech. Avoid "chopped prose": a sentence broken at random points is not a poem. Avoid poetic filler and archaic phrasing. Compress, and cut any word that isn't working.
