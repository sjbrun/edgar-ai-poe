# Style: Sonnet

## Hard rules
- Fourteen lines
- Iambic pentameter: five da-DUM feet per line, about 10 syllables. An occasional extra unstressed syllable at the end of a line (a feminine ending) is fine
- A fixed rhyme scheme from one of the variants below
- A *volta* (turn), a shift in argument, mood, or perspective

### Variants
| Variant | Rhyme scheme | Shape | Where the volta falls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shakespearean (English) | ABAB CDCD EFEF GG | three quatrains and a couplet | at line 9, and/or at the closing couplet |
| Petrarchan (Italian) | ABBAABBA + CDECDE or CDCDCD | octave and sestet | at line 9, where the sestet begins |
| Spenserian | ABAB BCBC CDCD EE | interlocking quatrains and a couplet | at line 9 or the couplet |

If the user doesn't name a variant, default to Shakespearean and say so in the notes.

## Conventions
- The octave or the quatrains set out a problem, question, or situation. The sestet or couplet answers, resolves, or overturns it
- A Shakespearean couplet often works as a summary or epigram that can stand alone
- Develop a single extended argument or metaphor across the poem
- Modern sonnets may loosen the meter or use slant rhyme, but they keep the 14 lines and the turn

## Themes & imagery
Love (often idealized or unattainable), time and mortality, beauty and decay, faith, fame and art's power to outlast death, and political or moral argument.

## Tone & vocabulary
Reasoned and intense, like an argument made with feeling. Keep the syntax natural. Don't invert word order ("the sky so blue was she") just to reach a rhyme. Archaic diction ("thee," "thou," "doth") only when the user asks for a period voice.
