# Sonnet Examples

## Example 1 (William Shakespeare, Sonnet 18, 1609, public domain): Shakespearean
```
Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?          A
Thou art more lovely and more temperate:         B
Rough winds do shake the darling buds of May,    A
And summer's lease hath all too short a date;    B
Sometime too hot the eye of heaven shines,       C
And often is his gold complexion dimm'd;         D
And every fair from fair sometime declines,      C
By chance or nature's changing course untrimm'd; D
But thy eternal summer shall not fade,           E
Nor lose possession of that fair thou ow'st;     F
Nor shall Death brag thou wander'st in his shade, E
When in eternal lines to time thou grow'st:      F
So long as men can breathe or eyes can see,      G
So long lives this, and this gives life to thee. G
```
**Why it works:** The first two quatrains list summer's flaws. The volta at line 9 ("But thy eternal summer...") turns the argument: the beloved won't fade. The couplet then reveals why, which is that the poem itself preserves them. One metaphor carries the whole poem.

## Example 2 (William Wordsworth, "The World Is Too Much With Us," 1807, public domain): Petrarchan
```
The world is too much with us; late and soon,
Getting and spending, we lay waste our powers;—
Little we see in Nature that is ours;
We have given our hearts away, a sordid boon!
This Sea that bares her bosom to the moon;
The winds that will be howling at all hours,
And are up-gathered now like sleeping flowers;
For this, for everything, we are out of tune;
It moves us not. Great God! I'd rather be
A Pagan suckled in a creed outworn;
So might I, standing on this pleasant lea,
Have glimpses that would make me less forlorn;
Have sight of Proteus rising from the sea;
Or hear old Triton blow his wreathèd horn.
```
**Why it works:** The octave (ABBAABBA) diagnoses the problem: materialism has cut us off from nature. The volta comes a half-line early ("It moves us not. Great God!"), and the sestet (CDCDCD) moves from complaint to an impassioned wish. Placing the turn mid-line makes the outburst feel spontaneous.
